# VAL1_BUSINESS_ASSESSMENT_SCRIPT_V0

Purpose:
Operator script for a $150 Val1 Business assessment.

The assessment sells clarity and scope, not automation. It is not implementation, not client onboarding, not a runtime setup, not a promise to automate the business, and not a separate Val platform. Use it to map workflow, pain, tools, privacy/risk, and the first realistic implementation path.

Tone:
Spanish-first, practical, founder/operator, business-clear, honest, no hype.

---

## 1. Positioning

Val1 is the business offer route on top of the same Val operating layer.

Plain Spanish:

“Val1 no es otra plataforma separada. Es la ruta de negocio de Val: primero entendemos tu proceso, dónde se pierde trabajo, qué herramientas usas, qué riesgos hay, y después decidimos si tiene sentido montar un flujo operador.”

What the $150 assessment does:

- maps the current workflow
- identifies operational pain
- reviews current tools and handoffs
- surfaces privacy/risk boundaries
- selects 1-3 candidate workflows
- separates what can be done now from what belongs later
- recommends a path: Val1 fit, not fit, later fit, or custom scope

What it does not do:

- no implementation during the call
- no automation promise
- no onboarding activation
- no client profile setup
- no integrations
- no production data migration
- no guarantee that every requested feature is ready

Client-facing line:

“La evaluación nos dice si Val puede ayudar tu flujo real antes de fingir que vamos a automatizar todo.”

## 3. 45-60 Minute Call Agenda

### 0-5 min: Relationship / Context Opening

Goal:
Set the frame and reduce hype.

Script:

“La idea de esta evaluación no es venderte un chatbot ni prometer automatizar todo. Quiero entender tu proceso real: dónde se pierde seguimiento, qué herramientas usas, qué parte te consume tiempo, qué datos son sensibles, y si Val puede ayudar en un flujo concreto.”

### 5-10 min: What Val Is / What It Is Not

Say:

- Val is a founder-beta operating layer with Telegram as the first cockpit.
- Val1 is the business route: workflow assessment, process mapping, and guided operator implementation.
- Sensitive actions stay behind confirmation.
- Current limits matter.

Do not say:

- Val is public self-serve SaaS.
- Val can run the business alone.
- Val includes every integration.
- Val guarantees OCR, DOCX, dashboards, or autonomous execution.

### 10-25 min: Current Workflow Walkthrough

Ask the client to describe one real workflow from start to finish.

Capture:

- trigger
- inputs
- people involved
- tools used
- handoffs
- documents/lists/statuses
- decisions
- follow-ups
- output / done state

Operator prompt:

“Llévame por un caso real desde que empieza hasta que queda cerrado. No me lo cuentes bonito; dime dónde se traba.”

### 25-35 min: Pain Points And Bottlenecks

Identify:

- what gets lost
- what repeats
- what is manual
- what depends on memory
- what gets delayed
- what creates stress
- what causes customer/vendor/team friction

### 35-45 min: Tools / Process Map

Map current tools:

- WhatsApp / Telegram
- email
- Excel / Google Sheets
- notes
- calendar
- folders / Drive
- CRM / ERP / accounting tools
- screenshots/photos/PDFs
- memory / verbal handoff

Ask:

- where does status live?
- who updates it?
- what is the source of truth?
- what gets duplicated?
- what breaks when someone is busy?

### 45-50 min: Data / Privacy / Risk Boundaries

Ask:

- What data should Val not store?
- Is there customer/vendor data?
- Is there financial, legal, medical, accounting, tax, or regulated data?
- Are there credentials, passwords, private IDs, or confidential documents?
- What actions must always require human confirmation?
- What would be dangerous to automate?

### 50-55 min: Choose 1-3 Candidate Workflows

Pick workflows that are:

- narrow
- repeatable
- valuable
- low enough risk for founder-beta
- not integration-heavy at first
- measurable within month 1

Good candidates:

- customer/vendor follow-up tracking
- meeting prep and next actions
- task/reminder capture
- document/status tracking
- simple list/inventory workflow
- weekly operator summary

### 55-60 min: Define Month-1 Success And Next Steps

Ask:

- What would make this feel useful within 30 days?
- What would save time or stress?
- What would make you pay for month 2?
- What must not happen?

Explain next-step options:

- no fit
- Val0 Personal if it is actually personal/admin
- Val1 Business $300/month base
- custom scope
- defer / later fit

## 4. Discovery Questions

Use the questions that fit the call. Do not interrogate mechanically.

### Work Gets Lost

- Where does work get lost today?
- What falls through the cracks?
- What do you remember only because it is in your head?
- What do people ask you for repeatedly?

### Weekly Repetition

- What repeats every week?
- What do you manually chase?
- What status do you check again and again?
- What update do you wish appeared automatically, but safely?

### Tools And Memory

- What lives in WhatsApp, email, Excel, notes, folders, screenshots, or memory?
- Which tool is the closest thing to source of truth?
- Where does the process split across tools?
- What gets copied by hand?

### Follow-Up

- Who needs follow-up?
- What follow-ups affect money, clients, vendors, or deadlines?
- What happens when follow-up is missed?
- How do you know something is done?

### Documents / Lists / Status

- What documents matter?
- What lists or statuses matter?
- Which documents are sensitive?
- Is reading/extracting documents necessary, or is tracking status enough for month 1?

### Never Automate

- What must Val never do automatically?
- What requires owner approval?
- What requires professional review?
- What would create risk if wrong?

### People Involved

- Who touches the workflow?
- Who owns decisions?
- Who enters data?
- Who needs status updates?
- Is this one-person operator support or a team workflow?

### 30-Day Value

- What would save time in 30 days?
- What would reduce stress?
- What would reduce missed follow-up?
- What would make this worth continuing?

## 5. Workflow Scoring

Score each candidate workflow 1-5. Lower risk and lower burden are better.

### Value

1 = nice to have  
3 = useful but not urgent  
5 = clear time/stress/revenue impact

### Simplicity

1 = vague or messy  
3 = understandable with some guidance  
5 = narrow, repeatable, easy to pilot

### Risk

1 = high legal/financial/medical/customer risk  
3 = manageable with boundaries  
5 = low-risk organizational support

### Reusability

1 = one-off custom edge case  
3 = some reusable patterns  
5 = strengthens Val product modules

### Data Sensitivity

1 = highly sensitive/regulatory  
3 = mixed data, needs boundaries  
5 = low sensitivity or can use sanitized data

### Integration Burden

1 = needs deep third-party integration  
3 = can start manual/read-only  
5 = no integration needed for first pilot

### Support Burden

1 = high-touch, urgent, constant operator load  
3 = regular check-ins  
5 = light guided support

### Founder-Beta Suitability

1 = not suitable now  
3 = suitable with tight scope  
5 = strong founder-beta pilot

Recommendation rule:
Prioritize workflows with high value, high simplicity, low risk, low integration burden, and clear month-1 success.

## 7. Boundaries / Do-Not-Promise

Do not promise:

- uptime/SLA guarantees
- autonomous actions
- full integrations included
- full OCR/photo/DOCX reliability
- professional advice replacement
- team/enterprise security claims unless actually implemented
- complete CRM/ERP replacement
- dashboards/client portals unless scoped separately
- data migrations included by default
- “Val will run your business”

Safer language:

- “We can start with a guided workflow.”
- “Sensitive actions stay behind confirmation.”
- “If an integration matters, we scope it separately.”
- “If a document is stored but not read, Val should say that.”
- “This assessment finds the first useful workflow before we promise implementation.”

## 8. Closing Script

Spanish:

“Lo que hago después de esta evaluación es resumir el flujo, los dolores principales, los riesgos, y recomendarte uno de varios caminos: puede ser que Val1 tenga sentido, puede ser que no sea fit todavía, puede ser que sea algo para más adelante, o puede ser que necesite custom scope.”

“El paquete base de $300/mes tiene sentido cuando hay 1-3 workflows claros, soporte ligero semanal o quincenal, y un valor real en ordenar seguimiento, documentos, reuniones, pendientes o próximos pasos.”

“Custom scope entra si estamos hablando de integraciones, dashboards, migraciones, equipo/permisos, alto riesgo, o algo que requiere más responsabilidad operativa.”

“Y si no conviene seguir, también te lo digo. Prefiero vender claridad que vender humo.”

English fallback:

“After this assessment, I summarize the workflow, pain points, risks, and recommendation. The outcome can be Val1, no fit, later fit, or custom scope. The goal is clarity before implementation.”

## 10. Operator Notes

- Capture source notes separately from reusable product docs.
- Sanitize product learnings before moving them into product docs.
- Avoid storing private business data in product documentation.
- Do not create real client folders from this script alone.
- Do not create client profiles, routes, integrations, or runtime behavior from this script.
- Keep Val1 as an offer route on the same Val operating layer, not a separate platform.
- If the assessment reveals high-risk data, pause and scope carefully.
- If the client expects strong uptime, team permissions, integrations, or dashboards, treat that as custom scope or later roadmap.
- If the workflow is personal/family/admin rather than business, consider Val0 Personal instead.

Operating principle:
Sell a scoped path to useful workflow support. Do not sell imaginary automation.
